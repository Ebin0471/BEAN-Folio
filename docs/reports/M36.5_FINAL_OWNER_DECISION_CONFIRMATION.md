## 3. Technical contracts accepted with decisions #3 and #6

Approval of decisions #3 (manager modifications) and #6 (audit durability) includes acceptance of
the documented technical contracts in plan §A.2 and §A.3. The following contracts are therefore
ACCEPTED as documented, and none may be weakened or simplified by later edits:

1. **Four-step Serializable transaction for manager-targeted writes (plan §A.2).** Every
   manager-targeted consent-gated workforce write must run the full four-step sequence in the
   SAME `Serializable` interactive transaction: fresh consent read → authorization →
   workforce-record write → strict audit. No step may execute outside the transaction, and the
   requirement is NOT limited to consent grant/revoke.
2. **In-transaction revalidation (plan §A.2).** The same transaction-bound authorization must
   revalidate the manager's active OWNER/ADMIN membership and the target employee's active
   organization membership — the authorization is never satisfied by a pre-transaction check.
3. **Only verified `P2034` conflicts are retryable (plan §A.3 rule 3).** `P2034` — the
   documented transaction-conflict error — is the only retryable error; the retryable set is a
   fixed, documented constant.
4. **`P1008` and `P2028` are NOT retryable by error code alone (plan §A.3 rule 3).** They are
   broad error categories whose recorded planning-time SQLite probe signatures were proven NOT
   safely attributable to contention; their SQLite contention mappings remain PENDING and they
   fail closed.
5. **Whole-transaction bounded retries with idempotent audit effects (plan §A.3 rules 4–6).**
   A retry re-runs the ENTIRE transaction callback in a NEW transaction — including the fresh
   consent read and the strict audit — bounded to three attempts; an attempt that actually
   committed resolves on re-read to the truthful already-applied result without appending a
   duplicate audit row; retry exhaustion fails explicitly and reports no success.
6. **PostgreSQL runtime concurrency verification remains PENDING (plan §6 item 10, §10).** The
   SQLite-side mechanism verification is complete with exact signature capture, but no
   disposable PostgreSQL environment existed at planning time; SQLite results are never
   substituted for PostgreSQL verification, and cross-provider verification is NOT claimed.
7. **The proposed uniqueness fallback is NOT ready-to-implement (plan §A.3 rule 8).** Its
   status-only `GRANTED` predicate conflicts with consent expiry (an expired grant row keeps
   `status = 'GRANTED'` and a status-only predicate would block a legitimate later re-grant);
   any database-enforced uniqueness alternative requires a separately authorized migration
   decision with proven predicate and lifecycle compatibility.

## 4. Unresolved retention questions (decision #7 sub-decisions — remain OPEN)

Decision #7 was approved as split: the workforce-row cascade behavior and audit-event survival
with actor FK set-null are APPROVED as documented (plan §A.4 retention matrix). The following
retention sub-decisions remain **OPEN** and are carried with their existing plan wording
(§A.4 "OPEN retention decisions"); nothing in this confirmation invents, proposes or resolves a
retention period:

- **R1 — audit `meta` scrubbing after parent deletion**: the platform has no meta-scrubbing
  subsystem today; retaining meta identifiers after user/org deletion is the current house
  behavior. Whether workforce audit meta (which records `organizationId` / `subjectUserId` /
  `actorUserId` as strings) must be scrubbed on parent deletion is a privacy-policy owner
  decision.
- **R2 — `AuditEvent` retention period**: none is defined in the plan and the platform has no
  purge job; any retention period is an owner/legal decision, out of scope of the plan.
- **R3 — organization-deletion alternative**: RESTRICTing organization deletion while workforce
  consent history exists (archive-first) is technically possible but is NOT recommended (it
  diverges from the house cascade pattern and complicates tenant offboarding). Recorded for
  completeness; cascade is confirmed as the default by decision #7's approval.

R1 and R2 correspond to decision #7's (c) metadata-scrubbing and (d) retention-duration
sub-decisions; they, and R3 as recorded, remain OPEN until the owner decides them. They do not
block the recorded approval of #7's (a)+(b) behavior, and nothing in this confirmation resolves
them by implication.
